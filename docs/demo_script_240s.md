### PART 1: The Smallholder Challenge & 25-Year NASA Climatology (0:00 – 0:50 | 50s)

**Visual (0:00 – 0:25):**  
Title screen with NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 banner, Team Azoic Dhaka credits. Presenter clicks the **"ডেমো দেখুন (Guided Demo)"** button on the top-right of the Home overview. The 7-step guided story modal appears at **Step 1: The Smallholder Challenge**.
- *Click path:* Top bar -> Click **"ডেমো দেখুন"** -> Step 1 displays.

**Voiceover (0:00 – 0:25):**  
*"Hello! We are Team Azoic from Dhaka, Bangladesh. We are competing in NASA Space Apps 2026 for the challenge: 'Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data'. Meet Abdul, a smallholder farmer cultivating 1.0 hectare in Godagari, Rajshahi. For generations, Abdul has followed a rigid Boro–Fallow–Aman rotation: pumping groundwater for winter rice, leaving his soil bare for 75 days in pre-monsoon heat, and relying on erratic monsoon rains for Aman."*

**Visual (0:25 – 0:50):**  
Presenter presses the Right Arrow key (or clicks **"পরবর্তী (Next)"**). Step 2 opens: **25-Year NASA Climatology & Honest Satellite Data**. The modal displays the 25-year (2001–2025) daily precipitation series from NASA POWER and NASA GPM IMERG across Bangladesh's 64 districts.
- *Click path:* Press `->` or click **"পরবর্তী"** -> Step 2.

**Voiceover (0:25 – 0:50):**  
*"Step 2: Why single-season weather forecasts are not enough. Climate change is shifting the monsoon onset across Bangladesh. Rather than guessing, FieldShift grounds every recommendation in 25 years of NASA Earth observations: NASA POWER climatology, high-resolution NASA GPM IMERG satellite precipitation, and ISRIC SoilGrids. And we maintain absolute honesty: our IMERG cross-validation shows no false onset claims—every recommendation is backed by real observations."*

### PART 2: Groundwater Depletion & Multi-Season Optimization (0:50 – 1:40 | 50s)

**Visual (0:50 – 1:15):**  
Presenter advances to **Step 3: Groundwater Stress (NASA GRACE-FO)**. The modal visualizes the JPL RL06.3M mascon trend showing $-1.07\text{ cm/year}$ depletion in Rajshahi's Barind tract, contrasted with SMAP root-zone soil moisture anomalies.
- *Click path:* Press `->` -> Step 3.

**Voiceover (0:50 – 1:15):**  
*"Step 3: Ground-level physics. NASA GRACE-FO gravitational satellite telemetry confirms that the Barind aquifer in northwestern Bangladesh is declining at -1.07 centimeters every year. Abdul's diesel tubewell costs are soaring, and pumping more water is suicidal for the water table. To survive, Abdul cannot just change planting dates; he must adapt his entire multi-season rotation."*

**Visual (1:15 – 1:40):**  
Presenter advances to **Step 4: Multi-Season Crop Planning (6 Seasons, 3 Years)**. Screen shows the 6-period agronomic matrix (Rabi, Kharif-I, Kharif-II) evaluating Abdul's existing rotation against 3 NASA-optimized strategies (Balanced, Water-Saving, and Profit-Focused).
- *Click path:* Press `->` -> Step 4.

**Voiceover (1:15 – 1:40):**  
*"Step 4: The optimization engine. FieldShift solves a multi-period mathematical model across 6 consecutive planning seasons. It evaluates cash budgets, water pumping capacities, and crop-specific soil texture matrices from SoilGrids. In Rajshahi, the Water-Saving strategy replaces high-water Boro with low-water Mustard, fixing pre-monsoon bare soil with Mungbean, and planting rainfed Aman."*

**Visual (2:05 – 2:40):**  
Presenter advances to **Step 6: Two-Field Regional Divergence** and clicks the **"দুই জমি তুলনা (2-Field Comparison)"** button. The interactive side-by-side modal opens: **Rajshahi (Barind Terrace)** on the left vs **Sunamganj (Haor Basin)** on the right.
- *Click path:* Press `->` -> Step 6 -> Click **"দুই জমি তুলনা"**.

**Voiceover (2:05 – 2:40):**  
*"Step 6: The ultimate proof of honest data: Two-Field Regional Divergence. Watch how FieldShift's recommendation flips between regions! In drought-prone Rajshahi, GRACE depletion (-1.07 cm/yr) and terrace clay force a low-water Mustard rotation. But in Sunamganj's haor basin, 3-meter deep monsoon floods make Kharif farming impossible; instead, winter receding beels provide 1,200 mm of cheap surface water, making single-season Boro rice the only profitable and sensible choice (49,200 BDT/ha net profit with zero groundwater deficit). One algorithm, two completely different regional realities—pure physics, zero hardcoding."*

**Voiceover (2:40 – 3:10):**
