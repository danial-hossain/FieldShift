1. The Smallholder Dilemma in Bangladesh

Smallholder farmers in Bangladesh face a compounding climate bottleneck:

  • Aquifer Depletion: NASA GRACE-FO detects groundwater drop of -1.07 cm/year in the Barind tract due to winter Boro rice irrigation.
  • Pre-Monsoon Bare Soil: High summer temperatures leave fields fallow for 75 days, exposing topsoil to erosion.
  • Onset Shifts: Unpredictable monsoon rains make single-season weather forecasts unreliable for survival.

3. Proof of Physics: Two-Field Regional Divergence

FieldShift does not use hardcoded templates; its recommendations diverge dynamically based on real soil texture and hydrology:

Rajshahi (Barind Clay Terrace)
24.47°N, 88.33°E | Area: 1.0 ha | Severe Aquifer Depletion (-1.07 cm/yr)

Baseline Practice: Boro Rice → Fallow → T.Aman (Severe tubewell diesel cost)

Recommended: Mustard → Mungbean → T.Aman Rice

Benefits: Saves ~809 mm irrigation water, fixes +35 kg/ha biological nitrogen, +৳ 155,000 profit delta (illustrative).

Sunamganj (Haor Deep Basin)
25.07°N, 91.40°E | Area: 1.0 ha | Deep Monsoon Flood Fallow

Baseline Practice: Boro Rice → Monsoon Deep Flood Fallow

Recommended: High-Margin Boro Rice → Flood-Fallow

Benefits: Utilizes 1,104 mm surface water from receding beels; +৳ 49,200 net margin while avoiding flood risk.
